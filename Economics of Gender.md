## Presentation

This course aims at providing participants with a thorough knowledge and understanding of the burgeoning literature on the economics of gender. Gender inequality in paid and unpaid work has diminished over time in most countries, thanks to the increasing participation of women in the labour market, the educational achievements of women, their increased representation in the political arena and elsewhere, which has been made possible also by the passing of laws of equality of opportunity by gender, the progress made in health and home technology, and so forth. Nonetheless, gender gaps persist in wages, promotion, career perspectives and recognition by peers, which are, at least in part, driven by explicit and implicit gender biases, and by deeply rooted cultural norms and stereotypes. Which policies can help reduce these persistent gaps? For example, how effective are gender quotas in achieving gender equality?
This course focuses especially on research methods in gender economics , especially covering recent work published in top economics journal, to provide students with the tools to fully master the literature and hopefully, do themselves excellent research.
In particular, the areas covered are:
gender stereotypes and biases;
research methods to measure and impact implicit and explicit gender biases;
the roots of gender inequality;
LGBTIQ+ economics;
gender based violence including domestic violence, workplace sexual harassment, bullying, retaliations and backlash; ;
The exam involves critically commenting a short paper, drawing from the lessons learned in the classes.
The lecture slides, the key papers referenced in the course, and past exam copies (including the best student answers) will be made available to the students in a dedicated coursework drive.
