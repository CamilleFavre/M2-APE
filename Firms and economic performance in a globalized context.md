## Presentation

This course focuses on the role of firms in shaping countries’ performance in a globalized context. The availability of firm-level data has opened a new avenue for research in recent years, the role of firms has received a growing attention and appears to be key. New facts that are simply unobservable at the aggregate level have emerged. Remarkably, a handful of large firms account for most of the job, output creation and international activity in high-income and developing countries alike and these few superstar firms appear to drive phenomena as varied as aggregate fluctuations, income inequality and comparative advantage in trade. The effectiveness of policies in terms of economic performance depends directly on their impact on these firms, but also more generally on their ability to reallocate resources to relatively more efficient firms. This process can, however, be hampered by distortions that constrain the activity of incumbent firms but also affect the selection, i.e. entry and exit, of firms.
This course will document the empirical approach to uncover what is known as granularity, and discuss new findings highlighting its importance for analysing economic performance and for understanding policy repercussions. The question is how granularity affects the effectiveness of policies aimed at supporting economic performance: Should policies target only high-potential firms, or would it be more effective to pursue general reforms that minimize distortions?
This course is composed of two interventions. Anne-Célia Disdier introduces the notion of firm heterogeneity and presents what are called superstar firms and their key characteristics before reviewing studies on how trade and industrial policies frame firms’ activities in globalization. Sandra Poncet examines how individual firms shape macroeconomic performance through three key dimensions: the determinants of microeconomic performance, the transmission of trade shocks, and spillovers from foreign firms.
