## Presentation

Office hours, Fabrice Etilé: Monday 12:30 am - 16:30 pm and Thursday 14:30 pm - 18:30 pm, send a mail to get an appointment.
Presentation
This course will cover selected issues in the analysis and the regulation of consumer behaviours and consumer markets. It will include the presentation of key economic mechanisms and theoretical concepts, empirical techniques, and a presentation of topics at the frontier of research (by PhD students). Examples are drawn from economic studies of food behaviours and policies, with a specific focus on the regulation of their health and environmental impacts (e.g., obesity, GHG emissions etc.).
Lecture 1 – Consumer behaviours and public policies: a framework
Lecture 2 – Neo-classical approach to consumer behaviour: household production theory
Lecture 3 – Empirical modelling for (price) policy analysis: demand for quantity
Lecture 4 – Empirical modelling for (price) policy analysis: demand for quality and firms’ strategic reactions to public policies
Interlude Lecture– Craig Pesme (PhD student) on Price and Welfare Measurement
Lecture 5 – Experimental and behavioural approach to consumer choices and consumer policies
Lecture 6 – Economics of advertising
In two sessions, students will present a literature review and a study protocol. Details of the syllabus can be found on the course’s website.
Targeted audience
This is an elective course at the crossroad of consumer economics and other fields in applied economics. The course is specifically intended to be complementary to courses on health behaviours and health inequalities, economics of well-being, public economics, environmental economics, industrial organisation and empirical industrial organisation. It may be chosen by students registered in the “Economics of Human Behaviour”, “Labour and Public Economics” or “Regulation, Environment, Market” research groups.
Understanding the arguments that are used in the design and analysis of policies aiming at regulating consumer behaviours is important for students interested in any issues where consumer behaviour plays a critical role. As such, this course can be useful both for students envisioning to pursue a PhD in health, environmental or consumer economics, and for those who would like to work in public institutions or NGOs in charge of sustainability and development issues. The course may also be of interest if you want to work later as a data analyst as I will offer insights on how to exploit consumer data.
Evaluation (groups of 2-4 students, depending on attendance)
Presentation of a literature review and a protocol (50%) + writing of a research proposal based on the two presentations and feedbacks (50%)
The literature review will have (1) to introduce some key stylized facts (2) to focus on the analysis of a key empirical paper published in a top economic journal (presentation, critical appraisal), complemented by the review of at least 3 related articles in economics, and possibly complemented by papers published in other fields (law studies, political sciences, health or environmental sciences, experimental psychology, experimental philosophy etc.) (3) to conclude on possible extensions.
The study protocol will have to describe the main hypothesis, the method (experimental or econometric), the data, the expected results, the expected contribution. Bonus points will be given to protocols based on an explicit theoretical framework, and to protocols based on structural econometrics methods or on RCTs designed so as to test mechanisms.
Examples of topics:
a. Price policies,
b. Environmental preferences and consumer behaviours,
c. Social inequalities and the distributional effects of public policies,
d Behavioural public policies,
e. Dynamics and change in preferences.
