## Presentation

Participation in the class is mandatory. The student presentations in class are part of the evaluation, and students need to actively participate in discussions during classes.
The course “Advanced Topics in Environmental Economics” will cover a selection of issues on the economic analysis of environmental policy, all of recent scientific interest and policy relevance. It will introduce you to some of the seminal models in the literature as well as to current research on the issues, and it will contain both theory and empirical applications. (In spring 2025, exceptionally, all classes are taught by Katrin Millock.)
In each class, students (potentially in groups of two) will have to present a research paper that will be assigned the week before. Each student will have to make an oral presentation of about 20mn, with a support (slides, pdf format) which will have to be sent to the instructor in the morning of the day of the presentation.
The first course will take place on 20th January. Student presentations start in the following course (Monday 27th January). (The schedule for presentations can be found above, among the general documents. If you have not yet signed up for a date, email me to do so.)
All readings are in the syllabus, which will be updated regularly on the APE website. The articles listed under each topic in the reading list are but a small selection of research on the issue, obviously. Articles marked with an asterisk should be read by everyone, whereas the other articles are an inroads into the subject for those who are interested in the particular topic. The general structure of each class is to go through one main theoretical model and then provide background and further material (empirical tests, for example) as optional reading. The topics are indicative and subject to change.
