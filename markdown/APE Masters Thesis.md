## Presentation

In the "Lectures" section, you will find the master theses from last year which obtained a grade equal or higher than 16/20. (updated 27/10/22)
The M2 masters thesis is carried out under the supervision of a research supervisor. Students are encouraged to contact an instructor working on topics of interest to the student and to discuss possible thesis topics. Instructors also propose each year topics that students may wish to pursue.
The final choice of a masters thesis supervisor must take place, at the latest, in January of the M2 year. A masters thesis proposal (1 page maximum), aproved and signed by the research supervisor, must be submitted to the programme administration at the latest at the end of December.
All APE instructors can serve as research supervisors for the masters thesis. If your thesis subject justifies it and with the approval of the director of the APE programme, you may choose a supervisor from outside the course. In this case the rapporteur that you will chose for the defense must be a teacher from the APE course.
In any case, the choice of the rapporteur (referee) must be approved by the Direction of the APE programme.
At least one of the two (supervisor or referee) must be a researcher from JOURDAN.
The masters dissertation counts for 15 ECTS.
IMPORTANT! The masters dissertation is graded on a scale of 0 to 20. The defense lasts roughly 45-60 minutes before a jury composed of the masters dissertation and a rapporteur.
After the defense, the defense jury proposes a grade for the dissertation that is transmitted to the masters office. However, the final grade is attributed and validated ONLY by the end of the year jury on the basis of the grade proposed by the defense jury, the dissertation, and,for grades of 15 and higher, the jury report.
In order to enter the PSE-Doctorate following the Masters programme, the average grade of the coursework in M2 year must be at least 14/20 and the masters thesis grade must be at least 15/20.

Here is some advice regarding your thesis prepared by the Director of the APE Masters:
Before starting your master thesis, you need to define a question that really interests you (you will have to spend quite a bit of time thinking about it) and that, by relating it to a broader topic, you can make interesting for your readers.
Your master thesis will:
1. Present the subject, i.e. the question. A good research question is typically one that you can answer with « yes » or « no ». You need to spell out clearly the hypothesis you are setting out to test.
Provide background information that makes the question interesting and relevant. The problematic needs to be clearly spelled out.
2. Summarize existing literature and results on this topic. Underline the points on which you wish to contribute.
3. Present your methodology, including the assumptions underlying the methodology, and defend the assumptions. Then clearly discuss results, their interpretation, caveats, and implications.
4. Discuss :
A master thesis will look like a paper to be submitted for publication, except that it might be more developed on the methodology and less selective on results; Can have heavier annex section.
If you present an empirical paper, data must be carefully presented, tables should all be readable without the text (notes should give enough information for tables to be self-contained). Give a sense of the size of the effects.
Citations must be carefully mentioned.
The JEL codes must be listed on the cover of your thesis.
Plagiarism is obviously strictly prohibited and severely punished.
